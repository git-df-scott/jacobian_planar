# Target-side exclusion result, 2026-09-04

## Statement

Let F = (P, Q) be a Keller map of the complex plane that is not an automorphism,
D its geometric degree, S its non-properness curve (Jelonek: a union of
polynomial curves), and rho the monodromy of the unramified cover of C^2 - S
given by F. Then (S, rho) satisfies:

1. every meridian of S has at least one fixed point and at least one moved point;
2. chi(X) = 1: D(1 - m + nu) + sum_i n_i(1 - k_i) + sum_p s_p = 1, where s_p is the
   number of sheets fixed by the image of the local link group at the singular
   point p;
3. every component of the escaping curve R in the normalisation is a copy of C,
   unramified over the smooth part of S, with one place at infinity
   (Orevkov's structure theorem for the regular extension);
4. no line meets S in a way that leaves a component of F^-1(line) homeomorphic
   to C (Nguyen 1999, Lemma 2.2), and generic fibres are connected with chi <= -1;
5. the source is simply connected: Stab(1) is the normal closure of the meridian
   lifts on staying sheets.

Computation. For each curve S below, pi_1(C^2 - S) was computed by braid
monodromy (root tracking, Zariski-van Kampen), all transitive representations
of degree 4 to 8 (to 10 or 12 where marked) were enumerated with GAP's
low-index subgroup algorithm, and conditions 1 to 5 were tested. The
enumeration is exhaustive for each curve and degree range.

Result. No configuration satisfies conditions 1 to 5. Every curve with only
ordinary cusps and nodes fails condition 2 by exactly one. Every curve
carrying a (2,5)-cusp with an ordinary cusp and a node passes conditions 1, 2
and 4 in exactly one representation (A5 on six sheets, the icosahedral rotation
group on the six vertex axes, meridians double transpositions with two fixed
points, staying counts 1 at the (2,5)-cusp and 0 elsewhere) and fails 3 and 5:
the escaping curve has two components with Euler characteristics 0 and -1, and
H_1 of the source is Z^2, generated by loops around the escaping branches.

Consequence for the sweep mechanism. The dimension-three counterexamples of
Alpoge and Gallagher (Gao, arXiv:2608.00222) sweep the tangent lines of a
plane curve K and twist with a third coordinate; the fibre sizes are d+1,
d-1, d-2, d-3 over generic points, smooth points, cusps and nodes of K. In
the plane these sizes give exactly the S4 configuration on the two-cusp
quartic with staying counts (1, 0, 1) and chi(X) = 0. The plane analogue of
the mechanism misses by the Euler characteristic of the missing factor.

## Structural facts established along the way

- If S is irreducible and homeomorphic to C it is quasi-homogeneous up to an
  automorphism (Zaidenberg-Lin), its complement group equals the local group
  at the singular point, and condition 2 fails. This is Nguyen's theorem.
- If S has only nodes and is transverse at infinity, its complement group is
  abelian, the action is regular, and condition 1 fails.
- For a = t^3 - 3t the only two-cusp curves with a single node are types [3,3]
  and [5,3]; all other cusp pairs admit only graphs. Two nodes collapse the
  group to near-cyclic in every instance.
- Condition 2 with cusps and nodes only: the Euler count is 1 + n(c - 1) - nu(e - n)
  minus the staying counts; nodes force shared fixed points whenever 2e > D,
  which is the mechanism of the off-by-one.
- The Euler-passing representations are Coxeter-type: the local relations at
  a (2,k)-cusp are dihedral of order 2k, at a node commuting; the spherical
  diagrams H3 (icosahedral) and A3 (tetrahedral) are what the enumeration
  finds, and only H3 balances at D = 6.

## Source side

`../trees/trees.py` enumerates boundary trees of compactifications of C^2 by
blowups of boundary points, computes the canonical multiplicities k_i and the
intersection matrix, solves for pole orders from dicritical data, and tests the
Keller inequality m_i + n_i + 1 >= k_i with geometric degree at least 2. No tree
with at most six blowups admits such data (11465 trees).

## What this does and does not say

It says: no plane Keller counterexample has a non-properness curve among the
configurations listed, at the geometric degrees listed. The list covers every
three-strand polynomial curve with cusps and nodes and non-abelian complement,
the four-strand curves with three cusps, and a range of two- and three-component
configurations. It does not say anything about non-properness curves with more
singular points, higher-strand projections, or geometric degree above 12, which
is where a counterexample must live if one exists.
